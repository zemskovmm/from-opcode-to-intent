# B11 METR evidence and limits

Supporting data. Attached to 11 The bottleneck moved.

The 2025 study randomized AI access for 246 real tasks from 16 experienced open-source developers working in familiar repositories. AI-allowed work took 19% longer; participants expected a 24% speedup and later believed they had been 20% faster.[2]

The original page now explicitly warns that these are historical results and should not be read as the current impact of AI on developer productivity.[2]

The February 2026 follow-up says newer tools likely helped more, but its data weakly identifies the magnitude: developers and tasks with high expected AI value selected out, pay changed, and concurrent agent work complicated time measurement.[3]

Do not present refusal to work without AI as proof of objective speedup. Do not promote the follow-up's raw estimates into an uncontested new effect size. The authors themselves warn about their interpretation.[3]

Use the pair as a lesson about measurement and changing bottlenecks. These sources are the PDF's historical evidence sequence, not a comprehensive survey of every later study.

If rehearsal runs long, trim forecasts and methodological detail, never the date or follow-up caveat.
