# B12 Learning and evaluation evidence

Supporting data. Attached to 12 Understanding is still expensive.

The January 2026 Anthropic study recruited 52 mostly junior engineers who knew Python but were unfamiliar with Trio. The experiment paired a coding task with an immediate quiz. Average quiz scores were 50% with AI assistance and 67% without; completion time was slightly lower with AI, but the difference was not statistically significant.[4]

The difference is 17 percentage points. The article's headline wording “17% lower” should not become an ambiguous “17% less mastery” slide. Prefer the actual two scores.[4]

The task and outcome are narrow: immediate learning of an unfamiliar library, not long-term engineering ability or every way of using AI. Explanation-seeking and conceptual questioning were associated with better quiz performance in qualitative subgroup analysis; that comparison was not an independent causal test.[4]

The gulf-of-execution/gulf-of-evaluation terminology concerns acting toward a goal and understanding a system's state.[5] Applying it to coding agents is our explanatory interpretation, not the finding of the learning experiment.

Return to the main argument: verification requires a mental model, and more generated output does not automatically supply one.
